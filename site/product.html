The learning loop

A tutor for the work beneath the answer.

Socrates keeps the conversation moving until an idea is yours: question it, explain it, recall it, and connect it.

A practice, not a feed

Not one more answer. A way to make one answer useful.

Every exchange is designed to leave a trace you can revisit: the claim, the reason, the uncertainty, and the next question.

Begin a conversation

01 / Dialogue

Start with uncertainty

Stay with the part that does not make sense yet.

Ask in ordinary language. Socrates follows your reasoning, offers a sharper question, and helps you find the assumptions hiding underneath an answer.

02 / Recall

Return at the right time

Bring an idea back before it fades.

Turn useful moments into small prompts for later. Revisiting an explanation in your own words makes the next conversation more precise.

03 / Knowledge map

Make connections visible

See the thread between the things you are learning.

Your questions form a growing map, so a concept in statistics can meet an old idea from philosophy or a note from yesterday’s reading.

04 / Practice

Test the edges

Check for understanding without performing certainty.

Use low-stakes questions and short explanations to discover what holds, what is missing, and where a better question should begin.

Built for continuity

Today’s question should improve tomorrow’s starting point.

Socrates is intentionally calm: a durable place for your thinking, rather than another stream asking for your attention.

What a session leaves behind

The session is the artifact.

Each conversation writes itself into a small, durable file you can revisit. The question, your first attempt, the sharper sub-questions, and the place this idea now lives in your map. Nothing is wasted in a feed you cannot find again.

A / Question

The starting sentence

The honest edge you typed in.

The exact prompt that began the conversation is preserved with the rest of the session, so the path back to an idea is always visible.

B / Sharper questions

What the conversation became

The questions Socrates returned to you.

Useful sub-questions are saved alongside your responses, so a later review can pick up the thread without re-reading the whole exchange.

C / Your attempt

The piece you wrote back

The explanation in your own words.

Your own short summary is the most honest signal of what you understand. It is kept next to the tutor’s response, so progress is visible.

D / Connections

The map entry

The place it joins the rest of your map.

When a concept touches something you already asked about, Socrates writes the connection into your knowledge map for the next time.
