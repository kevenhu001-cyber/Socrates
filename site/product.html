The learning loop

One question. Four ways to make it yours.

01 / Dialogue

Stay with the part that doesn't make sense.

Bring a question, a document, or an unfinished explanation. The tutor follows your reasoning and asks for the next step, rather than ending the conversation at the first answer.

Find your starting point →
A question that opens the next step

Your starting point

“The test is accurate, so the result must be right.”

Accurate in which direction? Does “most people with the condition test positive” tell us how many positive results are true?
Name the assumption. Then try explaining it again.

02 / Recall

Bring an idea back, not the whole transcript.

Return to a useful question after the conversation. Practise retrieving the idea in your own words, instead of mistaking a familiar explanation for something you can use.

04 / Practice

Find the gap before the exam does.

Test an explanation with a short question. Use feedback and your mistake book to see which part holds up, and which part deserves another conversation.

Compare practice features →

An example of working through a mistake

Explain your reasoning

Why can a reliable test still produce many false alarms?

“Because the test isn't perfectly accurate.”
That's a start. Now consider how many people don't have the condition. What happens when that group is much larger?
